How much can I overpay without a penalty?

Most UK lenders let you overpay up to 10% of your balance each year while you’re on a fixed or tracker deal. Go over that and you’ll usually pay an early repayment charge (ERC) on the extra. Some lenders measure the 10% against the original loan or reset it on your deal anniversary, so check your mortgage offer.

Once you’re on the SVR, there’s normally no limit.

Why does my payment jump after the deal?

When a deal ends, lenders move you to their standard variable rate, which is usually well above deal rates. This calculator works out the balance left when your deal ends and recalculates the payment at the SVR for the remaining years.

Most people remortgage before this happens, so treat the SVR figure as the cost of doing nothing.

Overpay or keep the payment the same?

These figures assume you keep your normal payment and pay extra on top, so the mortgage finishes sooner. Some lenders instead lower your monthly payment after an overpayment. You save less interest that way, so ask your lender to reduce the term if finishing early is the goal.

Should I overpay or save?

Overpaying earns you a guaranteed return equal to your mortgage rate. A savings account can beat it if its rate after tax is higher, and money you overpay is hard to get back, so most people keep an emergency fund first.

What loan to value means

Loan to value (LTV) is your mortgage balance as a percentage of what your home is worth. A £240,000 mortgage on a £300,000 home is 80% LTV. Lenders price their deals in LTV bands, and the lower your band, the lower the rate they usually offer.

The usual bands

Most UK lenders set rates at 60%, 75%, 80%, 85%, 90% and 95% LTV. The biggest rate differences tend to be at the higher end, between 95%, 90% and 85%, but moving from 75% to 60% can still save a meaningful amount over a five-year fix.

Getting into a lower band

  • Overpay before you remortgage. A lump sum or regular overpayments in the months before your deal ends can tip you into the next band. Check your overpayment allowance first, using the allowance calculator.
  • Get an up-to-date valuation. If prices near you have risen, your LTV may already be lower than you think. Lenders use their own valuation, so use a realistic figure.
  • Time it. Your balance falls every month, so a band you’re just above now may come within reach by the end of your deal.

How do I calculate my loan to value?

Divide your mortgage balance by your property’s value and multiply by 100. A £180,000 balance on a £250,000 home is 72% LTV.

What is a good loan to value for a mortgage?

The lowest rates are usually at 60% LTV or below. Each band down, such as from 80% to 75%, usually brings a lower rate.

Does overpaying reduce my loan to value?

Yes. Overpaying reduces your balance, which lowers your LTV and can move you into a cheaper band when you remortgage.

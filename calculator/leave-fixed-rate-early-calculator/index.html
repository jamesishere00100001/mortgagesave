How the comparison works

Leaving a fixed deal early usually means an early repayment charge (ERC), a percentage of the balance you repay. The calculator compares the two choices over the months left on your current deal: staying put, or paying the charge plus the new deal’s fees to move to the lower rate now. After your deal ends you’d be free to switch anyway, so that’s the period that matters.

Where to find your charge

Your mortgage offer lists the ERC for each year of the deal, often stepping down, for example from 5% in year one to 1% in year five. Your lender’s app or annual statement usually shows the current figure, and many lenders will tell you the exact amount over the phone.

Cheaper ways to get a lower rate

  • Lock in a new deal early. Most lenders let you pick your next deal three to six months before the current one ends, with no charge. See the fixed rate ending calculator.
  • Overpay instead. Overpaying within your 10% allowance cuts the balance the higher rate applies to, without any charge.
  • Moving home? Many fixes are portable, so you may be able to take your rate with you and avoid the charge.

Is it worth paying an early repayment charge to remortgage?

Only if the interest you save on the new rate, over the time left on your current deal, is more than the charge plus the new deal’s fees. With a large charge or little time left, it rarely is.

How is an early repayment charge calculated?

Usually as a percentage of the balance you repay, set out for each year of the deal in your mortgage offer. It often falls each year.

Can I avoid the early repayment charge?

You can usually avoid it by waiting until the deal ends, keeping overpayments within the yearly allowance, or porting the deal if you move home.

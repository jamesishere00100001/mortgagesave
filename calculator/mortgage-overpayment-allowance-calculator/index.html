The 10% overpayment rule

Most UK fixed and tracker deals let you overpay up to 10% of your balance each year without an early repayment charge (ERC). It’s a lender rule rather than a law, so the details vary:

  • What the 10% is of. Most lenders use the balance at the start of each year of the deal. A few use the original loan, or the balance on the day you overpay.
  • When the year resets. Often on your deal’s anniversary, sometimes on 1 January.
  • What counts. Regular monthly overpayments and one-off lump sums usually share the same allowance.
  • Unused allowance. It rarely carries over to the next year.

Your mortgage offer or your lender’s app will say which applies to you.

What happens if you overpay more than 10%?

The ERC is charged on the amount over the allowance, not on the whole overpayment. Charges are usually 1% to 5% and step down each year of the deal, so going over late in a deal costs less. Once your deal ends and you move to the standard variable rate, there’s normally no limit and no charge.

Turning the allowance into a monthly figure

Dividing the allowance by twelve gives the largest regular overpayment you can make all year without going over. If you’ve already overpaid this year, the calculator spreads what’s left over the remaining months.

Can I overpay my mortgage by more than 10%?

Yes, but during a fixed or tracker deal most lenders charge an early repayment charge on the amount above the allowance. On the standard variable rate there is usually no limit.

Is the 10% overpayment allowance per year or for the whole deal?

It is per year of the deal with most lenders, and unused allowance usually does not carry over.

Does a lump sum count towards the 10% allowance?

Yes. Regular overpayments and one-off lump sums normally share the same yearly allowance.

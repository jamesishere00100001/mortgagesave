How stamp duty is worked out

Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) is charged in bands, like income tax: each rate only applies to the part of the price inside that band. Since 1 April 2025 the rates for a home in England or Northern Ireland are:

  • 0% up to £125,000
  • 2% from £125,001 to £250,000
  • 5% from £250,001 to £925,000
  • 10% from £925,001 to £1.5 million
  • 12% above £1.5 million

First-time buyer relief

If you and anyone buying with you have never owned a home anywhere, you pay nothing up to £300,000 and 5% from £300,001 to £500,000. Above £500,000 the relief doesn’t apply at all and you pay the standard rates on the whole price.

Second homes and buy-to-let

Buying an additional property adds 5 percentage points to every band, from the first pound, for properties of £40,000 or more. If you’re replacing your main home and sell the old one within three years, you can usually claim that surcharge back. Buyers who aren’t UK resident pay a further 2%.

Scotland and Wales

Scotland has Land and Buildings Transaction Tax and Wales has Land Transaction Tax, with different bands, so this calculator doesn’t apply there.

When it’s due

Stamp duty must be paid within 14 days of completion. Your solicitor or conveyancer normally files the return and pays it from the money you send them.

How much stamp duty do first-time buyers pay?

Nothing on the first £300,000 and 5% on the part from £300,001 to £500,000, as long as the price is £500,000 or less. Above that, standard rates apply to the whole price.

How much is stamp duty on a £300,000 house?

£5,000 for a home mover in England or Northern Ireland, nothing for a first-time buyer, and £20,000 for an additional property.

Do I pay stamp duty on a second home?

Yes. Additional properties of £40,000 or more pay 5 percentage points on top of each band. You can usually reclaim it if you sell your previous main home within three years.

What happens when a fixed rate ends

When your fixed or tracker deal finishes, your lender moves you onto its standard variable rate (SVR) unless you’ve arranged something else. SVRs are usually several percentage points above the best deals, so the payment can jump by hundreds of pounds a month. This calculator works out the payment on the balance and term left, at each rate.

When to start looking

Most lenders let you choose a new deal with them (a product transfer) up to three to six months before your deal ends, and remortgage offers from other lenders usually last about six months. Starting early means you can lock in a rate and still switch if a better one turns up. Leaving it until after the deal ends means paying the SVR for every month it takes to arrange.

Stay with your lender or switch?

A product transfer is usually quicker, with no new affordability checks or valuation. Switching lender can get a lower rate but may come with legal and valuation work. Compare deals on the total cost over the fixed period, including arrangement fees, not just the rate.

Overpay before you remortgage

If your balance is close to a loan-to-value threshold (often 60%, 75%, 80% or 85%), overpaying before your deal ends can move you into a cheaper rate band. There’s usually no charge for overpaying once you’re on the SVR. The overpayment calculator shows the effect.

What happens if I do nothing when my fixed rate ends?

Your lender moves you to its standard variable rate. You can usually still move to a new deal afterwards, normally without an early repayment charge, but you pay the higher rate in the meantime.

How early can I lock in a new mortgage deal?

Most lenders let you choose a product transfer three to six months before your current deal ends, and many remortgage offers are valid for about six months.

Is the SVR always higher than a new fixed rate?

Almost always. SVRs are set by each lender and are typically well above the rates on new fixed and tracker deals.

How a lump sum overpayment works

A lump sum goes straight off your balance, so every month after it you pay interest on a smaller amount. This calculator follows your mortgage month by month: your current rate until your deal ends, then the rate after it, with the lump sum paid in the month you choose.

Finish sooner or pay less each month?

After an overpayment, most UK lenders either keep your monthly payment the same, so the mortgage ends earlier, or recalculate it lower over the same term. Keeping the payment the same saves more interest, because the balance falls faster. Lowering the payment frees up money each month, which can matter more if your budget is tight. Your lender will usually let you choose, but some pick one by default, so ask.

Watch the 10% limit during a fixed deal

Most fixed and tracker deals let you overpay up to 10% of your balance a year without a charge. A lump sum counts towards that, along with any regular overpayments. Above it, an early repayment charge (often 1% to 5%, falling each year of the deal) applies to the extra. The overpayment allowance calculator shows how much you can still pay this year. Waiting until the deal ends, when there’s normally no limit, can avoid the charge altogether.

Before you pay a lump sum

  • Keep an emergency fund. Money paid off a mortgage is hard to get back.
  • Pay off dearer debts, like credit cards or car finance, first.
  • If you’re close to a lower loan-to-value band (say 75% or 60%), a lump sum before you remortgage can get you a better rate as well.

Is it better to pay a lump sum off the mortgage or overpay monthly?

The same total saves more interest when it is paid sooner, so a lump sum now beats spreading it over the year. Monthly overpayments are easier to fit around a budget and to keep within the 10% allowance.

Do I have to tell my lender about a lump sum overpayment?

Usually yes. Many lenders want you to pay it by bank transfer or debit card through their website or app and to say whether to reduce your term or your payment.

Does a lump sum reduce my monthly payment?

It depends on your lender. Some lower the payment automatically and some keep it the same and shorten the term. You can normally ask for either.

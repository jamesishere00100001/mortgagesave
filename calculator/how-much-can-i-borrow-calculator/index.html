How lenders decide what you can borrow

Most UK lenders start with an income multiple: typically about 4 to 4.5 times your gross yearly income, or your combined income for a joint mortgage. Some lend up to 5 or 5.5 times for higher earners or certain professions. This calculator shows that range. It’s a starting point, not an offer.

What else lenders look at

  • Affordability checks. Lenders test whether you could still pay if rates rose, using your actual spending, other debts and childcare costs.
  • Your credit record. Missed payments or high card balances can lower what you’re offered.
  • Your deposit. A bigger deposit means a lower loan to value and usually a better rate, but it doesn’t raise the income multiple.
  • Income type. Bonuses, overtime and self-employed income are often only partly counted.

Don’t forget the costs of buying

Budget for stamp duty, legal fees, a survey and moving costs on top of the deposit. The stamp duty calculator works out the tax for England and Northern Ireland.

How much can I borrow on a £40,000 salary?

At a typical 4.5 times income, about £180,000. Some lenders go to around £160,000 at 4 times or higher for some borrowers, depending on your spending and credit record.

Is mortgage borrowing based on gross or net income?

Income multiples use gross (before tax) income. Lenders then check affordability against your actual take-home pay and spending.

Can I borrow 5 times my salary?

Some lenders offer 5 to 5.5 times income, usually for higher earners or certain professions, and often with a bigger deposit.

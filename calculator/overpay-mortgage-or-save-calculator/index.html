How the comparison works

Every pound you overpay stops being charged interest at your mortgage rate, so overpaying earns you, in effect, your mortgage rate with no tax and no risk. Savings earn the account’s rate, minus any tax on the interest. The calculator compares the two over the same period, with interest added monthly, and assumes both rates stay the same.

Tax on savings interest

The Personal Savings Allowance lets basic-rate taxpayers earn £1,000 of interest a year tax-free and higher-rate taxpayers £500. Additional-rate taxpayers get none. Interest in an ISA is always tax-free. If your savings interest is already above your allowance, choose your tax rate above.

It’s not just about the rate

  • Access. Savings are there if you need them. Money paid off your mortgage usually isn’t, unless your mortgage allows payment holidays or borrowing back.
  • Emergency fund first. Most guidance suggests keeping three to six months of spending in easy-access savings before overpaying.
  • Dearer debts first. Credit cards and loans usually cost more than a mortgage.
  • Remortgaging. A lower balance can move you into a cheaper loan-to-value band at your next deal.
  • Allowance. During a fixed deal you can usually overpay up to 10% of the balance a year without a charge.

A middle route is to save during your deal and pay a lump sum off when it ends, when there’s usually no overpayment limit. The lump sum calculator shows what that saves.

Is it better to overpay my mortgage or save?

If your savings rate after tax is lower than your mortgage rate, overpaying usually saves more. Savings are easier to get back, so many people keep an emergency fund first and overpay with what is left.

Do I pay tax on savings interest?

Only above your Personal Savings Allowance: £1,000 a year for basic-rate taxpayers, £500 for higher-rate and nothing for additional-rate. ISA interest is tax-free.

Can I get money back after overpaying my mortgage?

Usually not. Some flexible mortgages let you borrow back overpayments or take payment holidays, so check with your lender.

How the comparison works

A 2-year fix is often cheaper to start with, but after two years you have to re-fix at whatever rates are then on offer, and usually pay fees again. A 5-year fix locks in one rate. This calculator compares both over the same five years on net cost: the interest you pay, plus fees, adjusted for any difference in the balance left at the end.

The break-even rate

The break-even rate is the re-fix rate at which both choices cost the same. If you think rates in two years will be below it, the 2-year fix comes out ahead. Above it, the 5-year fix wins. Nobody knows where rates will be, so it helps to ask how far they’d have to move for your choice to go wrong.

Things the numbers don’t show

  • Certainty. A 5-year fix protects your budget for longer.
  • Flexibility. Early repayment charges usually apply for the whole fix, so a 5-year deal is costlier to leave if you might move or want to switch.
  • Loan to value. After two years your balance is lower and your home may be worth more, which can put you in a cheaper band when you re-fix. See the loan to value calculator.

Is a 2-year or 5-year fixed mortgage better?

It depends on what rates do. A 2-year fix wins if you can re-fix below the break-even rate after two years. A 5-year fix wins if rates are higher then, and gives more certainty.

Should I include fees when comparing mortgage deals?

Yes. A lower rate with a high fee can cost more than a slightly higher rate with no fee, especially on smaller loans. Compare the total cost over the same period.

Can I leave a 5-year fix early?

Usually only by paying an early repayment charge, often a few percent of the balance, which falls each year of the deal.
